Overview

A cane that watches the path ahead.

Traditional white canes rely on touch and sound reflection for obstacle awareness. Smart Cane augments that experience electronically with two ultrasonic sensors, adaptive vibration, voice warnings, and automatic fall alerts over Wi-Fi.

All intelligence runs on a single ESP32 microcontroller, keeping the design affordable, adaptable, and ideal for student, hobbyist, and NGO-driven assistive technology projects.

What’s inside

Built for dependable obstacle awareness.

Every sensor and output is arranged so the cane feels familiar while offering a powerful new layer of guidance and safety.

Dual-zone obstacle detection

Two ultrasonic sensors at different heights distinguish low curbs from chest-height obstacles and scale feedback across three distance zones.

Adaptive haptic feedback

The grip pulses faster and stronger as obstacles approach, giving an intuitive, silent cue before sound engages.

Spoken distance alerts

A DFPlayer Mini issues brief, clear warnings without cutting off existing voice prompts.

Guidance buzzer

A buzzer near the cane tip reinforces voice and haptic cues, helping users steer around obstacles once they are detected.

Fall detection

An MPU6050 motion sensor confirms a fall using impact and tilt thresholds over a short hold window.

Emergency Wi-Fi alert

When a fall is confirmed, the cane alerts nearby caregivers over Wi-Fi and retries automatically until it succeeds.

Behind the build

Real challenges, practical solutions.

Everything from sensor reliability to fall confirmation shaped the Smart Cane design.

  • Ultrasonic readings vary on soft, angled, or reflective surfaces, so thresholds require real-world tuning.
  • Distinguishing a fall from a hard tap or set-down required careful impact and tilt calibration.
  • Wi-Fi alerts depend on network availability, so alerts work best alongside a caregiver support system.
  • Continuous sensing, audio playback, and wireless comms put real pressure on battery life.
